Research checked **2026-10-21**. This is a detailed, source-linked collection of publicly accessible information, not a copy of the licensed case study. Dates below are **publication or event dates**, not the date this file was written. Company statements are identified as such; a contract ceiling, funding round, site count, and dataset observation are different measures and should not be added together as revenue or customers. Dollar amounts are escaped for Markdown rendering.

**For this repository's era-scoped debates:** only use an item if its original publication date is on or before the run's cutoff. Everything from 2020 onward is hindsight for the pre-COVID run; everything from 2022 onward is hindsight for the post-COVID run. This file is a research ledger, not an ingest-ready corpus source.

## History and operating milestones

- **Research roots, 2014–17.** MIT describes the Underworlds project, begun in 2014 under Eric Alm and Carlo Ratti, as the research predecessor. Matus worked in Alm's lab and Ghaeli in Ratti's group. The project drew a reported **\$4m Kuwait-MIT research grant** and about 20 researchers across six MIT labs. That was research funding for the precursor project, **not a Biobot equity round**. [MIT Spectrum](https://betterworld.mit.edu/spectrum/issues/fall-2018/running-start/), fall 2018.
- **Entrepreneurship path, 2017–18.** The founders used MIT DesignX, Sandbox, the MIT \$100K competition, and delta v; Biobot was on the [2017 delta v roster](https://entrepreneurship.mit.edu/accelerator/2017-delta-v/), pitched opioid monitoring at [Demo Day](https://news.mit.edu/2017/delta-v-demo-day-0912), and joined [YC W18](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/biobot-analytics). The founders had worked together on MIT wastewater research before company formation. [MIT impact study](https://entrepreneurship.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/delta-v-impact-study.pdf); [MIT profile](https://betterworld.mit.edu/spectrum/issues/fall-2018/running-start/).
- **Cary pilot, 2018.** Cary and Biobot used **10 manhole sampling sites** to observe opioid metabolites, including signals for fentanyl and naloxone. Cary's open-data description says each 24-hour aggregate represented roughly **4,000–15,000 people**, a deliberate privacy threshold. Its dataset reports category averages in **morphine milligram equivalents per day per 1,000 people**. [Cary open data](https://data.townofcary.org/explore/dataset/mayorschallenge_biobot-cary_2018-pilot-data_catchment-category-averages/api/); [2019 PNAS news feature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6442580/); [MIT Spectrum](https://betterworld.mit.edu/spectrum/issues/fall-2018/running-start/).
- **Cary outcome claim.** Biobot says six months of wastewater data informed outreach, medication disposal, and naloxone-related decisions; its case story attributes an **approximately 40% annual overdose reduction** to the broader city response the following year and reports prescription take-back rising from **924 to 2,511 pounds**. These are **company-reported before/after outcomes, not causal proof** that wastewater testing alone produced the change. [Biobot Cary case story](https://biobot.io/customer-success-story-biobot-and-cary-north-carolina/); [Cary open data](https://data.townofcary.org/explore/dataset/mayorschallenge_biobot-cary_2018-pilot-data_catchment-category-averages/api/).
- **Other early work.** A [2018 MIT repost of contemporary reporting](https://entrepreneurship.mit.edu/news/biobot-analytics-a-startup-using-sewage-to-fight-the-opioid-crisis-raises-2-5m/) mentions pilots in Boston, Kuwait, and South Korea as well as Cary. Pilot count is not contract count.
- **COVID pivot, 2020.** Biobot launched its pro-bono SARS-CoV-2 campaign on **March 23** with MIT, Harvard, and Brigham and Women's Hospital collaborators. It had **350 facilities in 40+ states** by late April, and contemporary reporting described **400+ facilities in 42 states** by May. The company moved to a paid COVID analysis service in June, according to the [Boston Globe's retrospective](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2022/02/04/magazine/those-covid-tracking-charts-start-here-with-biobot-sewage-analysis-that-knows-were-sick-before-we-do/). [Nantucket-hosted Biobot campaign sheet](https://nantucket-ma.gov/DocumentCenter/View/36910/Sample-Collection-April-28-2020-PDF); [April launch announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/biobot-launches-covid-19-detection-modeling-via-wastewater-analysis-301037713.html).
- **Commercial expansion, 2021–23.** The HHS and CDC work below turned wastewater testing into national-scale surveillance. Biobot said it had worked at **1,000+ locations** by [August 2022](https://biobot.io/press-release/biobot-analytics-launches-high-risk-substance-wastewater-platform-to-help-communities-combat-the-opioid-epidemic/) and that its CDC program contributed data from **400+ current locations in 250+ counties**, covering **60 million people**, in [February 2023](https://biobot.io/press-release/u-s-centers-for-disease-control-to-extend-national-wastewater-surveillance-system-contract-with-biobot-analytics/). The 1,000+ figure is cumulative; the 400+ figure is program scope at that time.
- **Broader menu, 2022–24.** Biobot [launched a high-risk substances platform](https://biobot.io/press-release/biobot-analytics-launches-high-risk-substance-wastewater-platform-to-help-communities-combat-the-opioid-epidemic/) in August 2022, [norovirus monitoring](https://biobot.io/press-release/biobot-analytics-to-begin-wastewater-monitoring-for-norovirus/) in April 2023, and a [respiratory panel](https://biobot.io/press-release/biobot-analytics-launches-respiratory-illnesses-panel/) for influenza A/B, RSV, and SARS-CoV-2 in August 2023. By [2025 reporting](https://www.cambridgeday.com/2025/11/24/biobot-tracks-expansion-into-new-markets/), the company was marketing private-sector disease dashboards as well as government testing.
- **2026 direction.** The ONDCP program extends substance-use monitoring nationally. Biobot's [current federal page](https://biobot.io/federal-government/) markets county/ZIP-level modeling and national security use cases; its [pharma page](https://biobot.io/pharma-life-sciences/) markets forecasts, clinical-trial targeting, and campaign planning. These are current offerings and claims, not evidence those segments have particular revenue.

This is a marketing catalog as of the check date, not proof that every target is in every customer's contracted panel or that all targets were available in earlier years. The site invites visitors to book a demo for full data access; it does not publish standard prices for these products.

## Public measurements and reusable datasets

## Technology and intellectual property

1. **Sampling and analysis.** For municipal work, participating plants collect wastewater, ship samples, and receive quantified pathogen or chemical signals. Biobot also describes building- or upstream-level sampling. Its service pages include dashboards, CSV export, API integration, and alerts. [Biobot treatment-plant page](https://biobot.io/wasterwater-treatment-plants/); [DCVC portfolio description](https://www.dcvc.com/companies/biobot-analytics/).
2. **Early opioid method.** A [2019 research paper indexed by PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31919800/) describes robotic sample collection and chemical analysis to estimate opioid exposure and treatment. Cary's [data dictionary](https://data.townofcary.org/api/v2/catalog/datasets/mayorschallenge_biobot-cary_2018-pilot-data_catchment-category-averages/attachments/cary_biobot_project_data_dictionary_pdf) documents metabolites, morphine-equivalent units, and sampling rules. A drug signal is aggregate and can be affected by prescribed use, disposal, or sewer dynamics.
3. **Issued sampling patent.** [US11714030B2, “Modular system for upstream wastewater sampling”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11714030B2/en), lists Biobot as assignee and was granted **2023-08-01**. A patent indicates a particular protected claim set; it does not by itself establish commercial exclusivity across all wastewater surveillance.
4. **Other application status.** [US20200385296A1, “Method for characterizing the prevalence of a biochemical condition within a population”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20200385296A1/en), describes concentration normalization, decay adjustment, and population prevalence estimates. Google Patents lists this **application as abandoned** in December 2024. Do not count it as an issued patent.
5. **Product software.** Biobot's [August 2023 accessibility report](https://biobot.io/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Biobot-Accessibility-Plan-Aug-2023.pdf) describes the Biobot Intelligence Platform as a web app for sample metadata submission and viewing analyses. This confirms a customer-facing software layer, but says nothing about software revenue share.

## Research and performance evidence

- A [2021/22 nationwide SARS-CoV-2 study with public input data](https://github.com/biobotanalytics/nationwide-trends-biobot-public) makes some historical measurements reproducible. It should be read with the paper's sampling and normalization details; the CSV is not the whole live Biobot network.
- The [2023 NIDA enrollment announcement](https://biobot.io/press-release/biobot-analytics-reaches-full-enrollment-for-nida-funded-nationwide-wastewater-based-monitoring-program-for-high-risk-substances/) reported **70 enrolled facilities in 43 states**, covering **about 35m people** and 62 counties. The [later 2026 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13441254/) analyzes **76 locations in 41 states** and **about 20m people**. These figures describe different reported program cohorts, coverage definitions, or analytical inclusion; do not merge them into one count without the study protocol.
- A [2024 Biobot/Mathematica research brief summary](https://biobot.io/press-release/wastewater-data-offers-powerful-tool-in-confronting-opioid-epidemic-new-study-reveals/) describes almost 40 weeks of data from **five counties**. Its Drug-SURGE alert algorithm reportedly flagged **71–100%** of suspected overdoses with **8–11 days** lead time in that small evaluation, and xylazine appeared in wastewater at least a month before reported overdoses. This is a limited study result, not a guaranteed production sensitivity or causal outcome.
- The [2026 Nature Water NIDA study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13441254/) evaluates a one-year national substance monitoring pilot. Biobot's [summary](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/findings-from-biobots-nationwide-wastewater-monitoring-program-for-prescription-and-high-risk-substances-published-in-nature-water-302809238.html) says the February 2024 fentanyl decline was identified **15 months** before it became clear in fatal-overdose reporting. The comparison depends on the reporting lag of that specific benchmark.
- Biobot currently claims its modeled outputs can reach every US ZIP and county weekly and be used to forecast disease trends. These are [product claims](https://biobot.io/pharma-life-sciences/); calibration, out-of-sample error, and paid utilization were not found in the cited pages.

## Business and market evidence, with limits

- **Growjo snapshot, unverified.** The [Growjo Biobot profile](https://growjo.com/company/Biobot_Analytics), checked 2026-10-22, reports **\$3.5m estimated annual revenue**, **33 employees**, **−59% employee growth over the prior year**, and **\$28m total funding**. The profile does not identify the revenue period, underlying financial statement, employee-count date, or funding-round reconciliation. Its revenue and headcount are third-party estimates, **not company disclosures or audited figures**. The funding total appears incomplete against Biobot's announced **\$20m 2021 Series A** plus its 2025 financing, for which Biobot did not disclose an amount. Do not use Growjo's estimate as ARR or as a current financial fact.
- **Revenue model.** Public descriptions point to recurring municipal monitoring, government contracts, and data/analytics sold to pharma and life-science customers. [Cambridge Day](https://www.cambridgeday.com/2025/11/24/biobot-tracks-expansion-into-new-markets/) reports a minimum municipal charge of **\$15,000 per year**. This floor cannot be multiplied by the cumulative number of tested sites to estimate ARR: many sites were pro-bono, federally funded, or part of one contract.
- **Government contract concentration.** The CDC program listed in the [commercial-contract compilation](https://nwbe.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/2024-05-01-Commercial-Testing-Contracts-NWBE-v2.pdf) was material in size, and the same document records a later **Verily** award. The [MWRA](https://www.mwra.com/biobot/biobotdata.htm) confirms the Massachusetts Biobot data stream ended in July 2026. These show procurement renewal and in-house substitution risk, not the company's complete 2026 customer situation.
- **Market expansion and competition.** Biobot now markets disease forecasting and substance-use intelligence beyond COVID; rival vendors, universities, and public laboratories also perform wastewater surveillance. The [Boston Globe's 2026 report](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/07/04/metro/wastewater-monitoring-biobot-massachusetts/) describes Massachusetts moving testing in-house and the CDC's earlier switch to Verily. A tested location is not proof of exclusive access or a durable contract.
- **Privacy and representativeness.** Cary's program aggregated thousands of residents per catchment; [Biobot's privacy page](https://biobot.io/about-biobot/) says its community data do not identify households. Wastewater coverage excludes people outside sampled sewersheds, and results can change with flows, inputs, or lab methods. A community chemical signal is not proof of an individual's conduct or the precise number of users.
- **What remains private.** No reviewed source supplies audited or company-disclosed revenue, ARR, gross margin, burn, cash balance, retention, active paid customers, per-sample price, 2025 post-money valuation, or segment revenue. Those variables require direct company disclosure or defensible public filings; a third-party estimate is not a substitute.

## Research findings and measurement cautions

- The [2026 NIDA pilot paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13441254/) reports that the wastewater fentanyl signal's decline was visible in February 2024, while fatal-overdose surveillance reflected it later. Biobot's [press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/findings-from-biobots-nationwide-wastewater-monitoring-program-for-prescription-and-high-risk-substances-published-in-nature-water-302809238.html) describes a **15-month lead**. That timing compares two specific series and reporting processes; it is not a general lead time for every substance or location.
- Wastewater concentration measures a community signal, not exact infections or drug users. Results depend on sampling geography, sewer coverage, dilution, shedding, assay performance, and site selection. The [CDC program description](https://www.cdc.gov/wastewater/about/) explains reporting scope; [Cambridge Day's customer interviews](https://www.cambridgeday.com/2025/11/24/biobot-tracks-expansion-into-new-markets/) describe one city using Biobot data as a complement to case data rather than a standalone forecast.
- Public sources above do **not** establish Biobot's current revenue, ARR, gross margin, retention, number of active paying customers, total contract backlog, valuation, or market share. The funding screenshots and press coverage should not be used to infer those metrics.
